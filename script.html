第 1 页 · Cover　⏱ 20 sec

💡 这页在讲：自我介绍 + 今天要讲什么

Hi everyone, I’m Ruilin.

Today I’ll talk about a paper called “Toward Intelligent and Secure Cloud.”

It’s from Southeast University in China.

The question is simple: can AI models, like ChatGPT, protect a cloud system by themselves?

Not just find an attack — but also plan a fix, run the fix, and get better over time.

I’ll cover four parts: the problem, how the system works, the results, and my own thoughts.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：The problem）

第 2 页 · The problem　⏱ 45 sec

💡 这页在讲：云很复杂、容易被攻击；传统防御太慢，要“主动防御”

First, the problem.

Today, the cloud runs almost everything — apps, 5G, smart devices.

But a cloud has many parts: networks, APIs, virtual machines, hardware.

More parts means more ways for attackers to get in.

This paper looks at one type of attack: DoS, or Denial of Service.

The attacker doesn’t steal data. They just flood the service so real users can’t use it.

Most old tools are reactive. They wait for the attack, and then respond. That’s often too late.

So the goal is proactive defense: catch the problem early, and act fast.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：Prior work）

第 3 页 · Prior work　⏱ 50 sec

💡 这页在讲：以前的方法各有缺点：要么只能报警，要么换个攻击就要重新训练

People have tried this before. Here are four groups.

One: tricks like Moving Target Defense and honeypots. They work, but each one is built by hand for one setting.

Two: monitoring systems, called SIEM. They watch and send alerts — but they don’t fix anything.

Three: reinforcement learning. It can learn a defense, but you must retrain it for every new attack.

Four: recent AI work in security. It usually does just one task, or needs a lot of human experts.

So what’s missing? One system that can watch, decide, act, and learn — for many kinds of attacks.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：The key idea）

第 4 页 · The key idea　⏱ 25 sec

💡 这页在讲：核心想法：一组 AI 助手分工合作，靠“记忆”变强，不用重新训练

Here’s the key idea in one sentence.

Use a team of AI agents. Each agent does one job. Together they cover the whole defense.

And when a new attack comes, they don’t retrain the model. They just remember what worked and what didn’t.

Three words to remember: end-to-end, no retraining, self-evolving.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：Threat model）

第 5 页 · Threat model　⏱ 25 sec

💡 这页在讲：攻击者能做什么、不能做什么

Quick look at the attacker.

The attacker can send three kinds of DoS attacks: SYN flooding, SlowHTTP, and Memory DoS.

They can also change their tactics over time.

But they can’t get admin access, and they can’t touch the defense system.

One key assumption: the monitoring data is trusted. Remember this — I’ll come back to it later.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：Architecture）

第 6 页 · Architecture　⏱ 55 sec

💡 这页在讲：整体结构：5 个 AI 助手，做成一个循环

This is the whole system. Five agents. Each one is just an AI model with its own instructions.

A — the Collector — gathers the data and cleans it up.

B — the Analyzer — checks how bad things are and gives a risk score.

C — the Decision-Maker — makes a plan.

D — the Deployer — carries out the plan. If no tool exists, it writes a new script.

E — the Feedback Giver — checks if it worked, and saves the lesson.

Then the loop starts again, with more experience. That dashed arrow is the “learning” part.

Let me go through each step quickly.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：Stages A and B）

第 7 页 · Stages A and B　⏱ 55 sec

💡 这页在讲：A 收集整理数据；B 给风险打分（0–10 分）

Step A, the Collector.

A cloud makes a huge amount of data in many formats. The Collector reads it, removes repeats, and merges similar events.

For example: two failed admin logins from the same IP, plus one normal heartbeat.

The Collector turns this into one alert — “repeated failed logins” — and drops the heartbeat as noise.

Step B, the Analyzer. It gives a risk score from 0 to 10.

The score has three parts: how much is affected, how bad it is, and how long it lasts.

The nice part: the AI also explains why. And it can spot strange patterns that no rule was written for.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：Stages C and D）

第 8 页 · Stages C and D　⏱ 65 sec

💡 这页在讲：C 做计划并检查每个动作；D 执行，没有现成工具就自己写脚本，先测试再上线

Step C, the Decision-Maker.

In a real attack, many things can go wrong at once. So it first breaks the big job into small tasks, and puts the most risky ones first.

A big worry with AI is that it can make things up. We call this hallucination.

So every action is checked. If a number is out of range, or it breaks a limit, the action is rejected.

Step D, the Deployer.

It first looks in a library of known defenses. If nothing fits, the AI writes a new script.

But before running it, the script is checked and tested in a safe sandbox.

If it passes, it runs — and it’s saved for next time.

The code on the right is a real example from the paper: it blocks one IP address and writes a log.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：Stage E）

第 9 页 · Stage E　⏱ 50 sec

💡 这页在讲：E 打分、判断成功失败、存进“记忆”；不改模型，只改提示

Step E is what makes the system learn.

First, it scores the result in five ways: security, recovery time, resources, money, and service quality.

Second, it decides: did this round succeed or fail?

Third, it saves this into memory.

There are two kinds of memory. Short-term: every step in this attack. Long-term: the most recent full attacks.

Here’s the important part: the AI model itself never changes. It just gets better instructions, with past lessons inside.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：Experiment setup）

第 10 页 · Experiment setup　⏱ 40 sec

💡 这页在讲：实验怎么做：3 个模型、3 种攻击、和 3 个强化学习方法比较；全部是模拟

Now, the experiments.

They tested three AI models: GPT-4o mini, and two open models — DeepSeek and Qwen3.

They tested three DoS attacks.

The cloud was simulated, with up to 100 pods and 500 virtual machines.

They compared against three reinforcement learning methods: DQN, Actor-Critic, and PPO.

They ran 200 tests, each with 10 rounds of attacks.

One thing to keep in mind: everything is a simulation — not a real cloud.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：Result 1: efficacy）

第 11 页 · Result 1: efficacy　⏱ 50 sec

💡 这页在讲：结果一：成功率接近 99%，比最好的旧方法高 14 到 43 分

Here’s the main result. Efficacy means: how often did it stop the attack?

For SYN flooding, LLM-PD gets about 99%. The best old method gets about 83%.

For SlowHTTP, it’s about 98% versus 85%.

Memory DoS is the big one. The old methods only get about 56%. LLM-PD gets almost 99%. That’s a gap of more than 40 points.

And it’s not just the best model — all three AI models beat all three old methods.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：Result 2: the cost）

第 12 页 · Result 2: the cost　⏱ 45 sec

💡 这页在讲：结果二：代价是更慢（最多慢约 200 倍）、有时更贵

But it’s not free.

The old methods take less than one second per action. LLM-PD takes about 3 to 12 seconds.

In the worst case, that’s about 200 times slower.

Cost is interesting. GPT-4o mini is the fastest AI here, but it’s the most expensive — about 3 dollars per round, because you pay for each API call.

The open models cost only a few cents, but they are slower.

The authors say it’s worth it for important systems. I partly agree — but DoS attacks can be very fast. I’ll come back to this.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：Result 3: self-evolution）

第 13 页 · Result 3: self-evolution　⏱ 45 sec

💡 这页在讲：结果三：越用越快（步数从 10–16 降到 5–6）；不同模型擅长不同步骤

Does the memory really help?

They counted how many steps it takes to stop an attack.

In round 1, it takes about 10 steps — or 16 for Memory DoS.

By round 10, it’s down to about 5 or 6 steps. So it really learns from experience.

Small note: the numbers in the text come from two different models. But their chart shows all three models getting faster.

On the right: each model is good at different steps. So a smart idea is to mix them — use the best model for each step.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：My thoughts）

第 14 页 · My thoughts　⏱ 70 sec

💡 这页在讲：我的看法：优点 + 五个问题（模拟、太慢、提示注入、自动跑代码、缺少消融实验）

Now, my own thoughts.

What I like: it covers the whole defense loop, not just one step. You can use any AI model. It learns without retraining. It checks every action. And the code is public.

But I have some questions.

One: it’s all simulated. We don’t know how it works on a real cloud.

Two: speed. A few seconds per action may be too slow for a fast flood.

Three: the AI reads logs directly. An attacker could hide a message inside a log — like in a username — to trick the AI. This is called prompt injection.

Four: the system runs code that the AI wrote. Is a sandbox enough for real use?

Five: they never tested the system without memory. So we don’t know how much the memory alone helps.

👉 翻页 →（下一页：Takeaways）

第 15 页 · Takeaways　⏱ 25 sec

💡 这页在讲：总结三点 + 谢谢 + 提问

To wrap up, three things to remember.

One: AI agents can run the whole defense loop — watch, judge, plan, act, and learn.

Two: with memory, the system gets better without retraining, and it beat the old methods on all three attacks.

Three: there are still open problems — speed, safety of AI-written actions, and real-world tests.

Thank you! I’m happy to take questions.
